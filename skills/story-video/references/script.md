# Writing the narration

The user's text (deck, brochure, feature list, brief) is **source material, not the script**.
Keep its facts and its promise; rewrite everything else for the ear and for the film's theme.

## A narrator tells, a slide lists
Slide text is fragments ("Tagihan SPP dicatat tangan."). Narration is a person talking to a person:
full sentences that lead into each other. Read the whole script aloud in one go — if it sounds like
someone reading bullet points, rewrite it.

| slide-style (don't) | narration (do) |
|---|---|
| "Pukul 06.45. Seorang ibu melepas tangan anaknya." | "Seperti ibu ini. Pukul enam empat puluh lima, ia melepas tangan anaknya di depan gerbang, melambaikan tangan, lalu berbalik pulang." |
| "Tagihan dicatat tangan. Nilai diketik ulang." | "Di ruang tata usaha, tagihan masih ditulis tangan… Sore harinya, para guru mengetik ulang nilai ke Excel." |

## Rules
1. **One throughline.** Pick one character or situation and return to it ("ibu tadi", "surat izin yang ia kirim pagi tadi"). Callbacks make separate scenes one story.
2. **Connective tissue.** Open lines with links that carry time and logic: *Dan, Padahal, Sementara itu, Sore harinya, Malam itu, Lalu, Di sini, Bahkan* (en: *And, Meanwhile, That evening, But, So*).
3. **Vary the rhythm.** Mostly flowing sentences; one short line where it lands ("Lalu… bagaimana jika gerbang itu tetap terbuka?"). Fragments only as deliberate emphasis, at most once or twice per film.
4. **Narration ≠ on-screen text.** The screen carries the short headline; the voice carries the story around it. Never read the slide.
5. **Numbers as story, not data dumps.** "Di balik gerbang itu ada lima puluh tiga juta anak…" rather than "Lima puluh tiga juta siswa." Keep the source on screen.
6. **Match the preset:** drama = documentary storyteller, unhurried; booth = warm presenter, clear; fun = friendly, a smile in the voice, talks to "kamu/Anda"; viral = one sharp hook sentence, then quick, direct.
7. **Write for the voice:** numbers and acronyms spelled as spoken; avoid words the voice mis-stresses; one emotion tag per line at most, at the start. Commas and "…" make natural pauses.
8. **Product only after the turn**, introduced like a character ("Inilah <Produk> — …" / "Meet <Product> — …"), then what changes for each person in the story, ending back on the opening image when possible.
9. **Only claims the source supports** — rewording must not upgrade a feature.

## Check before voicing
Read it aloud start to finish (or `edge-tts` a quick draft): does each line follow from the last?
Could a listener retell the story after one hearing? Show the full script to the user for approval —
voice credits are spent only on an approved script.
